First Law: Privacy Policy

Last updated: 23 November 2026

First Law is a puzzle game by l1tbo. This policy covers the game on Android and iPhone.

What we collect: nothing

First Law has no accounts of its own, ads, analytics or crash reporting. We never receive any information about you, from anyone of any age. The only thing the game sends over the internet is your progress, to your own Google account, and only if you use Google Play Games (below).

What stays on your phone

Your progress (levels finished, best move counts, your stats) and your settings are saved on your device, so you can carry on where you left off. Your settings never leave it. Uninstalling the game deletes what's on the phone.

Cloud saves with Google Play Games (Android)

On Android, the game signs in to Google Play Games for you if you use it, and you can sign in from the game's settings. While you're signed in, a copy of your progress (levels finished, best move counts, whether you used a hint, your stats and the level you last played) is kept in your Google account as a Play Games saved game, so it carries over to your other devices and survives reinstalling. Google stores it under Google's privacy policy; it's held in your account, not ours, and we can't see it.

Signing in gives the game an identifier for your Play Games account. The game keeps it on your phone only, so that if someone else signs in on the same phone, their progress and yours aren't mixed up. The game doesn't read your name, email address or anything else from your account.

If you don't sign in, nothing leaves your phone. To stop syncing, sign out of Google Play Games in its app; to delete the saved copy, use the Google Play Games app's settings. On iPhone the game has no cloud saves yet.

Vibration

The game uses your phone's vibration for haptic feedback. Android grants this to apps automatically, and the iPhone needs no permission for it. It reads nothing from your phone.

App stores

Google Play and the App Store handle downloads and may collect information under their own privacy policies. First Law receives none of it.

Changes

If this ever changes, for example because a future version adds cloud saves on iPhone, this page will say so before that version is released, with a new date above.
